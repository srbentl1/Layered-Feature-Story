Arizona's heat waves are becoming more intense and frequent, but residents are finding ways to cope, and the state is helping them. From hydration stations to bill relief programs Arizonans are not alone.

What living in the desert is really like and how we survive!

Living in Arizona, at times, it feels like you are living in an oven with no escape. You hear of and see videos of people leaving cookies on baking sheets in their cars for an hour, coming out to a fresh batch of cookies. You even see people having to buy bags of ice to help cool down their pools.

"It is only miserable three months out of the year, then it is amazing.” - Common saying from the citizens of Yuma, Arizona

Though that may be true for some areas, other areas suffer longer with the extreme desert heat. In areas like Yuma, Arizona, many times the heat wave lasts up to six months. The heat can start as early as May and last into October.

For the citizens of Yuma, what does that mean? Running air-conditioning units longer, staying indoors until it cools down enough to play or get stuff done outside, running pool pumps longer to keep them cool and algae-free, and the fear of higher electric bills! Let's take a look at what the state and cities are doing to help the citizens combat this heat.

Cooling Centers and Hydration Areas

The City of Yuma helps its citizens by providing cooling sites and hydration areas. The Yuma County website found specifically on Surviving Yuma's Heat | Yuma County page, maps out the amount of and locations of water and cooling centers. The cooling centers are areas that provide free air-conditioned spaces to help citizens escape the dangerous heat. Each location is different; some may provide free bottled water or only have access to water fountains. Some areas may also provide additional resources such as charging stations and free wifi.

Hydration areas don't provide an air-conditioned space to help cool citizens down, but they do provide bottled water to help them stay hydrated. Most of these locations are run by volunteers, but the city does provide paid staff and provide support to the volunteers.

Bill Relief and Home Weatherization

During the times of the heat wave, bills skyrocket from citizens paying 100 to 200 dollars during winter to paying 400 dollars plus during the heat wave. This massive jump affects what locals can afford, forcing them to cut back in other areas. Luckily, Arizona Public Service (APS) has multiple programs to help citizens stay on top of their bills and to lift the weight of the massive increase off their shoulders. The first major action APS takes is to halt all disconnections for past due bills during June to October, and they even waive any late fees accrued during that time. Next, they have an Energy Support program that allows citizens to apply for a monthly discount on their bill, which ranges from 25% to 60%. If customers require medical devices to survive, APS will provide a 35% discount on their monthly bill. There are various other programs that they offer, which could be found on Get Help Paying Your Energy Bill | APS page.

Those who want to try to improve their homes but have limited income can get assistance from the Arizona Department of Housing, which provides Home Weatherization. Home Weatherization, according to the Department of Housing Weatherization Assistance Program | Department of Housing page, provides support to income-eligible families to help drive down the energy cost to run their appliances. The work ranges from:

  • Thermal insulation, mostly attic insulation
  • Testing, tuning, and maintaining heating and cooling systems
  • Shading sun-exposed windows
  • Checking and reducing air conditioning duct leaks
  • Other energy conservation needs, which are identified by the auditor

Outdoor Support

The City of Yuma isn't just providing cooling zones or financial aid to utility bills, but they are also providing support by designing cooler neighborhoods. The city has mandated in its zoning laws that new commercial buildings and even residential developments must plant specific trees to provide shade to help lower ground temperatures, and keep the neighborhoods walkable during the extreme heat. The city has also installed new shade structures for highly populated parks and plans on continuing to install additional shading structures.
